# Modular cupboards and drawer connectors

The Desk collection has a cupboard alternative to drawers. Closed assembled width, including hinge hardware, is limited to 170 mm. Select 1–3 modules high and 1–2 columns across within that width. Each column needs at least 70 mm. Module height is 80–170 mm and depth 60–170 mm. Two 170 mm modules give a 340 mm cupboard. Every finished printable part is separately checked within 170 mm; assemblies may exceed the printer volume.

Floors and tops default to automatic: one floor below and one top above each vertical column, without intermediate floors. Each can instead be included in every module or omitted everywhere. Double-door cases include a 6 mm full-depth centre divider, so each leaf opens into its own tall bay. It supports the front and catches in the back-down print orientation without a large unsupported bridge. Single-door cases retain their full open width. Front stiles and optional magnet mounts have tapered support webs. Back walls and sides remain integral.

Single doors offer left or right hinges; double doors hinge at the outside of each column. Each module has printed hinge barrels integral with its case and door panel. Separate head-down printed pins have 2.8 mm shafts. Barrels have bed-side support flats/buttresses and bores using the selected circular clearance plus a print-friendly teardrop roof. Stop posts have tapered side-wall buttresses. The shorter 18 mm hinge units and 19.8 mm pins permit insertion/removal even in the shortest stacked modules. Pins are inserted head-up and gravity retained, not permanently locked.

Vertical door sections join into one moving leaf using two rear splice keys at every seam. Closed-ended dovetail tunnels retain each key; assemble the panels flat before presenting the whole door to the case and installing its pins. Case ports connect with removable split-stud snap clips with long in-plane spring arms pressed from the rear. Their retaining heads sit inside the cases; squeeze studs from inside to release. Internal grid joins are generated automatically. Additional top/bottom and side ports, plus spare keys, allow later connections. Rear clips project 2.4 mm beyond the requested body depth. Spare keys are included in downloads/gram estimates and shown only in printing-orientations view.

Stops allow about 110° on ordinary doors. Adjacent inner doors in side-by-side double-door columns use 90° stops and slim pulls to avoid collision when opened together. The preview uses the actual per-door axis/limit and moves panels, pulls and splice keys together. Flex catches and matching receivers are integral. Separate printed pulls use rigid quarter-turn T-studs. Insert with the head aligned to the slot, then turn the face groove vertical to retain; turn back to horizontal to remove. Optional paired 6.2 mm pockets accept user-supplied nominal 6 × 2 mm magnets; check polarity/fit before gluing.

Door surface styles and openwork use the shared style names. Pattern fields retain a solid edge frame, hinge/handle/catch surrounds and solid splice regions. Thin face grooves, enclosed splice channels and small horizontal bores still need slicer toolpath/bridge inspection; supports are not silently enabled.

Drawer units optionally gain vertical, side-by-side or both sets of rear connector ports and printed snap clips. Drawer bodies shorten by 2.4 mm to clear stud heads. Existing feet/pockets are retained: use locating-pocket units below and a smooth-top final unit above. Vertical pitch is unit height minus 1.2 mm. Match width, depth, row count, wall and connector settings. Remove drawers to release clips. Defaults without connectors retain the original drawer geometry.

All parts retain 8% gyroid, two wall loops, three top and three bottom layers, with zero minimum skin-thickness overrides. Filament estimates, guides, sidecars and recognised Bambu projects use the shared profile. No photo/coaster/keyring/nameplate profile changes.

Validation covers connected closed meshes, all style/openwork combinations, automatic/manual faces, left/right/single/double doors, closed width/part limits, rigid opening motion after catch release, physical stop engagement, smallest-stack pin insertion/removal, joined door/case alignment, magnet pads, snap-clip seated fits and drawer travel with actual clip heads. Snap engagement and flex catches intentionally need elastic deflection and require printed tests; digital rigid checks cannot prove retention, wear or strength. These are small desktop storage assemblies, not load-rated furniture. Test one hinge, connector and module before a large stack.
